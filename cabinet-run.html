The cabinet run

You've got one wall, one ceiling, and a rendering that doesn't know either number. Your tape says 93¾; the picture says trust me. Guess wrong and you pay for it in a second trip, a restocking fee, or a filler you end up cutting by eye in the driveway.

Type your two numbers and you get back the box widths that actually close — the filler sizes, the height stack that lands on your ceiling, and the exact words to type into the store's search. Before you fall in love with a layout the store doesn't stock.

Stock cabinets come in a handful of widths. A wall comes in one. The run is the set of stock boxes that fills your wall to the sixteenth — fillers where the wall isn't square, a height stack that lands on the ceiling.

Nothing you type here leaves your device. No account, no server, no analytics — your numbers live in this browser tab and are erased when you close it. "Start over" wipes them now. Nobody can read them.

Why this one exists

Last Saturday: a 93¾-inch mudroom wall, a nine-foot ceiling, a rendering that looked finished, and an AI chat that had invented a “window nook” off two numbers on my sketch. The tape didn't care about the picture. The math closed on its own — 1⅞ + 30 + 30 + 30 + 1⅞ = 93¾, 90 + 18 = 108 — and then the store's house brand turned out not to make that pantry in 30 wide. A different line did. The rendering wasn't a plan. The tape was.

Stock sizes are the alphabet

12 to 36 in 3-inch steps. Your wall gets spelled with those, plus a filler each end for the inch and a half no wall gives you square.

Seams stack, height is a stack

Uppers match the widths below so every seam lines up. Tall plus upper reaches the ceiling: an inch short is scribe, four is crown.

A window is a bay

One bay, open — drawers below, wall above. Never across a seam.

Part 1 — Measure the wall

Inches. Ceiling at both ends and the middle; type the smallest.

Part 2 — The window, if there is one

Skip if the wall is blank. Otherwise outside edge of the trim.
