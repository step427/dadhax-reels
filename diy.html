The Cleat Wall cut list

Measure your wall once and type in the width. Before you buy a sheet, you get:

  • Rail heights, and where each stud and screw lands
  • Every 45° rip, counted
  • How many sheets to buy

A 10 ft wall comes back as 9 rails, 12 rips at 45° and 2 sheets, with every joint on a stud.

Shop notes from my cleat wall

  1. Start at 2½ in, not 1½. Split it down the middle at 45° and one board gives you two hangers. The only waste is the kerf.
  2. Hangers fall off? Add a stop. A block under the cleat bears on the wall, so the load pushes in instead of prying out.
  3. Still shifts? Wedge it. Push a thin offcut in behind the stop. The scraps from ripping your 45s are right there.
  4. Every hanger is just a hook. A block with a 45° on the back and something to hook onto. Look at what's leaning in your corner.

My shop rules

More than ten years as a physical therapist. These are the three I actually follow in my own garage.

  • Ears and eyes on before the saw starts. Not after the first cut.
  • Hang the heavy stuff between hip and shoulder height. Lifting off the floor or over your head is where backs and shoulders get hurt.
  • Bring it close before you lift. The same weight held out at arm's length is much harder on your back.

Most of what I know about saw safety I learned from Stumpy Nubs on YouTube. Worth your time.
