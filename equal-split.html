The equal split

You want three equal pieces out of one board, and the board is 16-3/8 wide. Divide it straight, cut on your lines, and the blade eats an eighth at every cut. Your middle piece comes out short, and you find out when your shelves don't match. The blade is the part your math forgot.

Type your board's width and how many pieces. You get the fence setting with the blade counted, and the tape trick for when your phone is in the house. My offcut: 16-3/8, three shelves, an 1/8 blade. Fence at 5-3/8. Three equal shelves.

Why this one exists

The laundry room left me a butcher block offcut and I wanted three floating shelves out of it. On camera I said it out loud: "I'm not very good at math." So I didn't do any. I tilted the tape until 18 landed on the far edge, because "you bring it to a number that's easily divisible by three," and marked 6 and 12. Then I set the fence a hair short, at 5-3/8, because "I'd rather go short." Fresh blade, one pass a shelf: "right on the money." This page does the same thing, with the blade counted on purpose: Instagram, Facebook.

Part 1 — Your board

Measure the face you're splitting, edge to edge. Decimals are fine: 16-3/8 is 16.375, 3/32 is 0.094.

Filled with my offcut so you can see it work. A full-kerf blade is about 1/8, a thin-kerf about 3/32. Not sure? Cut a scrap and measure the slot.

Part 2 — Your cut

The split

this page doesn't know your saw Rip against a fence or a clamped guide, never freehand, and use a push stick once the piece gets narrow. Measure your own blade's kerf before you trust the number. Your saw's manual wins.
