The if-then card

Hard days come. Your jaw is tight, you're short with everybody, or you go blank, and that's the exact moment you have to decide what to do. So you don't decide. The scroll decides, or the fuse does.

The fix is to decide on a decent day, in if-then form. Across 94 tests, plans written as "if this happens, then I'll do that" had a medium-to-large effect on people actually following through. Tap your warning lights, your hard moments and what resets you. You walk out with a 3x5 card: your if-thens, your floor, two people and when to call a pro. Mine is already filled in below: if my jaw clenches, then 10 push-ups.

Nothing you tap or type here leaves your device. No account, no server, no analytics. Your card is erased when you close the tab, so print it or copy it.

this card isn't a crisis plan I'm a physical therapist, not your therapist. This is a card for ordinary hard days. Two weeks of bad sleep or dread is a doctor visit, not a card. Thinking about hurting yourself? In the US, call or text 988, any hour. In danger right now, call 911.
Why this one exists

My takeaway from a neuroscientist's podcast interview: "have a resiliency plan. Hard days come, hard moments come." So I made mine. Robot mode, for me, is a clenched jaw and going blank.

I made the plan on a decent day, ready for a hard one. If my jaw clenches, then 10 push-ups. It gets me back into my body instead of floating out in outer space. On camera I offered to send my plan to anyone who asked. This page is the version you don't have to ask for: Instagram, Facebook.

Part 1 — Your warning lights

How does your body tell you it's a hard day, before your head admits it? Tap every one you know.

Part 2 — Your hard moments

Which moments knock you over? Pick up to three. One plan per moment beats one plan for everything.

Part 3 — If this, then that

For each moment, pick the reset that works for you. Small and physical beats big and noble.

Part 4 — Your floor

The least you'll do on your worst day. Two is plenty.

The boulder question: your two people

One person is a single point of failure. Name two people you can tell when the lights come on.

And a second

Now make the call. Ask: “when you see me go quiet or short, can I come to you? You don't have to fix anything.”
