You're doing fineThat's the part that's confusing

The job is steady. The kids are healthy. You'd say you're grateful and you'd mean it. And still — somewhere between the driveway and the kitchen there's a weight you can't name, and it's been there long enough that you stopped trying to name it.

I started DadHax to make a dad's life easier. Hacks, shortcuts, the claw of a hammer carrying a full sheet of plywood one-handed.

Then it clicked why those matter: the good parts of a dad's life are supposed to be heavy. The marriage. The kids. The block you live on. The thing you keep "thinking over." That work is hard because it's the work that counts — an easier life was never the goal. A more powerful one is.

Lane one — make the trivial easy The bag becomes the bowl and nobody does dishes tonight — more minutes at the table. The stuff that doesn't matter should cost you nothing — that's what the everyday hacks are for.
Lane two — spend it where it's hard Every hour lane one buys back goes to the work that compounds: the dad, the husband, the useful man, the guy your block is glad lives there. The tools in the toolbox are built for that lane.
I'm Nick. Physical therapist for ten years, first flip under contract, a dad sorting it out loud. Everything here is free and ungated because it's just what I needed somebody to have written down five years ago.
  • 1.Open the toolbox. Pick the tool that names your stuck point.
  • 2.Give it ten minutes. Every tool ends with a step you can take tonight.
  • 3.Spend what it frees on the heavy stuff. That's the whole system.
