The last coat

You built the thing. It's Wednesday, it has to be in use Saturday, and the can says "recoat in 4 to 6 hours" like you've got nothing else going on.

So the last coat goes on tacky, and it comes out with swirl marks, drips at the edges, and a soft spot that prints the first thing you set on it. Finishing is where a good build gets ruined.

Pick the finish and the day you need it, and you get the clock: a time for every coat, the flip, 220 between them, and the hour it holds a laundry basket. A countertop in oil poly, three coats a side, needed Saturday at five: coat one goes on Wednesday at four.

Nothing you type here leaves your device. No account, no server, no analytics. Your dates live in this browser tab and are erased when you close it. "Start over" wipes them now.

Why this one exists

My shellac top took six coats, 220 between every single one, underside first. Sanding between coats is the whole trick: it knocks down the dust that landed while the last coat dried. This weekend, a laundry-room countertop: oil poly, every side, bottom first so the drips drip downward. I had company coming, I'd chilled the whole tub on an AI's advice, and coat two came out with swirl marks and drips at both edges. What are we learning here? Chill the roller, not the can. And count the hours before you open it. The six coats, in the shop: Instagram, Facebook.

The window is a floor

The can's recoat time is the least you wait, not a target. "Dry enough, a little tacky" costs you the coat. Wait longer and nothing is lost: you're sanding 220 between coats either way.
