2026-09-10 Catch them being kind

Watch it on Instagram →

First week of school. Kids don't stop growing.

One of the most high-leverage things we ever started in our house: catching them being kind. Not catching them fighting -- catching them being kind. Doing a chore without being asked. Showing a brother or sister some empathy. Each one earns a point.

Enough points gets the reward. When they were younger it was something they could touch -- a toy they wanted, sitting where they could see it, in a locked box. Carrot in sight. Out of reach. (We use dollars now.)

The toy isn't the point. The point is that one day they stop counting and it's just who they are.

If you want to go deeper, the book that did the most for us: How to Talk So Kids Will Listen & Listen So Kids Will Talk, by Adele Faber and Elaine Mazlish. I butchered the title on camera. That's the right one.

2026-09-09 Split a board dead center. No math.

Watch it on Instagram →
This one only exists there — Instagram stopped letting sites embed reels.

I don't trust my math. I inch myself every time -- measure, do the subtraction for the blade, cut, and it's off.

So I stopped doing the subtraction. Set the fence to roughly half the width, less the blade. Mark the board from one edge. Flip it end for end and mark it again from the other edge.

Now you've got two marks. Dead center is between them, and it's sitting right there on the wood where you can see it -- no tape, no fractions. Line the blade up to it and cut.

Whole stack came out split down the middle. Half of them never needed a second pass.

You'll forget this by Thursday. Follow and you won't have to.

2026-09-09 Start at 2.5 inches. Waste less.

Watch it on Instagram →
This one only exists there — Instagram stopped letting sites embed reels.

Making French cleat hangers, and this is the part I got wrong for a while.

You'd think you start with a skinny strip, because the hanger is skinny. So you rip an inch and a half, tilt the blade to 45, and cut. You get one hanger and the offcut is scrap -- a whole 45 gone.

Start at 2.5 inches instead and split THAT down the middle at 45. Now the same board gives you two hangers that mirror each other, and the only waste is the kerf in the middle.

The extra inch isn't for the hanger. It's slop -- for the blade, and for me not being a good enough woodworker to cut it tighter.

Every board you don't waste is one you don't buy. More like this, daily.

2026-09-09 This is why I don't need a shed

Watch it on Instagram →
This one only exists there — Instagram stopped letting sites embed reels.

A French cleat is one strip of wood cut at 45 degrees. Half goes on the wall, angle up. The other half goes on the back of whatever you're hanging, angle down. They hook together. That's the whole idea.

What it buys you is that nothing is permanent. Every holder lifts off and goes somewhere else in about a second -- no screws, no patching, no committing to a layout you picked before you knew how you'd actually use the space.

About 90% of my garage hangs on one now. That's the reason I've never had to buy a shed. The wall does the storing.

2026-09-08 Your blade isn't dull. It's dirty.

Watch it on Instagram →
This one only exists there — Instagram stopped letting sites embed reels.

I was cutting red oak and the board started smoking. My first thought was the obvious one -- dull blade, time for a new one.

It usually isn't. Most of the time it's buildup baked onto the teeth. Pitch and resin cook on, the blade stops clearing, and it starts burning its way through instead of cutting.

There's a proper way to fix that -- degreaser, a soak, a scrub brush. I didn't do the proper way. Drill, wire brush attachment, run it in reverse on one side so it spins through the teeth instead of against them, then forward on the other side. Two minutes and you can see metal again.

It doesn't get everything. It gets enough that the wood stops smoking.

Two-minute fixes, every day. Follow and your Saturday gets shorter.
