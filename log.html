No circle jig. A router and a stick.

Fix a straight offcut to the router base, put a pin through the other end into the center of the blank, and walk the router around. The distance from the pin to the bit is your radius.

Mine ran a 10 inch radius, so 20 inches across. Three tops, all the same size, no template.

That blank under the router is the side table top from earlier this week. The top is eight 45° wedges, the same cut that makes a French cleat hold a wall of tools.

It started the way a lot of my projects start: "Hey handsome husband, can you make me this?" and a photo.

Eight wedges, 45 degrees each, cut on the table saw against a template. Then glue up in stages. Eighths into quarters, quarters into halves, halves into one round top. Cut the circle, sand it, finish it.

Salvaged red oak, left-over finish, and a set of store-bought legs. Three of them came out of it, and they're in the house now.

The photo she sent me was the whole plan. If somebody has handed you one and it's still sitting in your phone, that is your weekend.
