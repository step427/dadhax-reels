Two small kitchen fixes.

Peanut butter: use a spatula, not a butter knife. It scrapes the jar edges clean so you use every ounce, spreads smooth with fewer crumbs, and the spatula comes out fairly clean too.

Butter: scrape across the top instead of cutting off chunks. Scrapings spread easier and more even.

2026-09-15 Same boulder, every day

The myth of Sisyphus. A king who cheated the gods, and cheated death, and got sentenced to push a boulder up a hill forever, only to watch it roll back to the bottom.

Most of us hear that as torture. I think the gods meant it that way.

Something I read flipped it for me, and it said it better than I can: if the goal shifts from finishing the task to becoming the kind of person who can move mountains, the gods accidentally gave Sisyphus the ultimate training ground.

So if you keep facing the same challenge over and over again, there's a good chance that's your superpower. You just haven't found the angle to see it that way yet.

Within great pain, one finds great purpose.

There's one of these every day. You're either getting them or you're not.

2026-09-14 Ask for spears

Heard this one on Simon Sinek's podcast, A Bit of Optimism: ask for spears.

Somebody has an idea. They do the research. They bring it to the group -- and then they ask everybody to throw spears at it. Poke holes. Find the weak spot now, while it's cheap.

That only works if you set your ego down first. The goal isn't being right. The goal is a better thing at the end.

Criticism still stings. So don't go looking for criticism -- go looking for growth. Same feedback, different question: How can I grow? How can I be better?

Try it once this week. Hand one idea to someone you trust and ask them to break it.

I do the listening. You get the one idea worth keeping. Daily.

2026-09-14 Your computer, from your phone

Watch it on Instagram →
This one only exists there — Instagram stopped letting sites embed reels.

The computer's at home, you're not, and the thing you need is on it.

Chrome Remote Desktop is Google's free tool for that. Set it up once:

1. On the computer (Windows 10+ or macOS 10.13+), go to g.co/crd/setup in Chrome or Firefox. 2. Install Chrome Remote Desktop, follow the setup, and pick a PIN. 3. Get the Chrome Remote Desktop app on your phone, same Google account. The computer shows up in the list. Tap it and enter your PIN.

Leave the computer on, since it can't connect while it's off.

2026-09-13 How drifting actually starts

Nobody wakes up and decides to waste a decade. That's not how it goes.

It goes like this: you do a thing with a little less purpose behind it than you had yesterday. Then a little less intention. There are degrees of it, and every single degree feels reasonable at the time.

That's drifting. It never announces itself. It just quietly takes the wheel.

The fix isn't dramatic. It's vigilance and diligence on the boring days, when nobody is watching and nothing is on fire. It's hard. It's also the only thing that works.
