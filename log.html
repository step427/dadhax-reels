Watch it on Instagram →

Finishing is where a good build gets ruined, and I went through three answers before I got there.

Dark stain on a sample: no. Matte poly: no. Shellac: yes.

Then it is just patience. Underside first at zero coats. Topside after that, 220 grit sanding between every single application. Six coats before I stopped.

Sanding between coats is the whole trick. It knocks down the dust that landed while the last coat dried and gives the next one something to grab.

A top made of eight 45 degree wedges shows every flaw you leave in it, so the finish has to be boring and repeatable.

Nobody films the boring half. I post it anyway, every day. Follow and you get it.

Inside corners on trim are where the gaps show up.

What I do on the cope:

1. Clean up the cope with a rotary tool until the profile matches. 2. Test-fit it against the corner. 3. Nudge it tight.

Take off a little, test, repeat. Sneaking up on it beats one big cut you can't undo.

Four knife basics from my kitchen, the ones that keep your fingers where they belong:

1. Pinch the blade for balance and control. 2. Flatten an edge of the food so it sits still on the board. 3. Claw grip. Save them fingers. 4. On an apple, cut in at an angle and twist. Removes the core, minimizes waste.

Try the claw grip on your next carrot. Go slow the first few times.

Food prep is a good third thing too. Hand your kid the peeler and you've got ten minutes side by side.

My kids finished a 1,000-piece puzzle, the third time we've done this one as a family, and asked me to frame it.

What I did differently this time: I glued the back to that brown floor-protection paper using wood glue. It gives the whole thing some rigidity. Then I put weight on it. A bag of dog food works.

2026-09-21 Dip your nail hangers so they don't mar tools

Watch it on Instagram →
This one only exists there — Instagram stopped letting sites embed reels.

The hangers on my French cleat wall are just nails. Two per block, and I can knock out forty of them in a day.

The part that matters is the coating. I dipped them in a rubbery paint, Flex Seal I think, so the nail doesn't mar the tool hanging on it. Bare steel against a drill chuck or a hammer handle leaves marks. Rubber doesn't.

Same two nails hold drills, hammers, pry bars, paint stirrers. One block, one pattern, the whole wall.
