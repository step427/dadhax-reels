2026-08-10 Willpower is finite — the Swiss cheese model

Watch it on Instagram →
This one only exists there — Instagram stopped letting sites embed reels.

The gift bags called me out. There WAS a system — and it's broken, because it ran on willpower.

A system that relies on willpower is bound to break. Willpower is finite. One bad day, one bad week, and the energy that was holding the whole thing together is gone.

So you don't build one perfect layer. You build layers, plural. In medicine it's called the Swiss cheese model: every layer has holes, but you stack them so the holes don't line up. More layers, fewer things get through — and when something breaks, it's an easy fix instead of a collapse.

Stop blaming your discipline. Start stacking layers.

2026-08-09 God-sized goals — the not-knowing is the point

If you already know how to reach it, it isn't a goal. It's a task.

I heard Dan Martell describe carrying goals so big he has no idea how he'd pull them off — and that the size is the whole point. Big enough that his ceiling ends up being somebody else's floor.

The part that got me wasn't the ambition. It was what comes after: you have to hand it over. Not the plan — you don't have a plan. The not-knowing.

I've spent years treating "I don't know how" as a reason to shrink the goal back down to something I could picture. He's saying that's backwards. Not knowing how is the evidence you finally picked a real one.

So pick the one you can't figure out yet. Then stop trying to figure it out before you start.
