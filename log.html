2026-09-20 Why your French cleats fall off the wall

Watch it on Instagram →

If your French cleats keep working loose, the problem is leverage, not the cleat.

Mine kept dropping off the wall, worst with angle brackets for shelving. The farther the weight sits out from the wall, the more torque there is trying to pry the hanger off the rail.

The fix is a stop: a block on the bottom of the hanger that bears against the wall underneath the cleat. Now the load pushes into the wall instead of levering the hanger away from it.

Want more out of it, drive a wedge in behind the stop. That locks it down tight.

Hanging a cleat wall is a good third thing, too. Do it with someone and you'll talk for two hours without trying.

2026-09-19 Break down a full sheet on the floor

Watch it on Instagram →
This one only exists there — Instagram stopped letting sites embed reels.

You don't need a table saw or a second set of hands to break down a full sheet of plywood.

Do it on the floor: 1. Lay a 4x8 sheet of foam board down first. It carries the whole sheet so nothing sags mid-cut, and your blade runs into foam instead of concrete. 2. Plywood on top, with the cut side hanging past the foam far enough to get a clamp on it. 3. Clamp a straight edge on your pre-marked line, set so the saw's shoe rides the edge and the blade lands where you want the cut. 4. Drop your blade depth so it passes just below the plywood, barely into the foam. Shallow means the foam survives for many more cuts and the underside of the cut comes out cleaner. 5. Walk the saw down the straight edge in one pass.

A straight edge, a couple of clamps and a sheet of foam is the whole kit. Check today's prices yourself, mine are three years old.

Breaking down sheets is a good third thing, too. Put someone on the far end and you talk while you work.

The full breakdown is on my YouTube (NicksDadHax), including getting the sheet down from overhead storage without dragging out a ladder.

2026-09-18 Joint boards without a jointer

Watch it on Instagram →
This one only exists there — Instagram stopped letting sites embed reels.

No jointer? You don't need one for a tight glue-up.

I tried three ways: 1. Table saw straight-edge jig. Took a lot of material off, but left the edge wavy. 2. Handheld power planer. Gaps along the joint, worst at the ends. 3. Router with a flush-trim bit riding a straight edge. Tight joints, just needs sanding.

Hands down the easiest was the router. For a big project with a lot of jointing, a cheap jointer will still save you time.

A glue-up is a good third thing: two sets of hands, one clamp job.

All three methods, and every joint up close, are in the full video on my YouTube (NicksDadHax).

Start your dust collection with your voice.

My setup: 1. Plug the miter saw and a small shop vac into a Wi-Fi smart power strip. Leave the vac's own switch ON. 2. Add the strip in the Alexa app and name it "miter saw." I have a smart speaker in the garage. 3. "Alexa, turn on the miter saw." The vac kicks on and the saw has power. You still pull the trigger. Or push the strip's button.

Do the math on paper before you buy the wood.

How I plan a project: 1. Pencil and grid paper. Draw it out, get the measurements, do the math. 2. List the stock you already have, then write your cut list. 3. Put the cut list into CutList Optimizer (cutlistoptimizer.com) and hit calculate. It works out the most efficient layout for your material. I use the web version, and there's an app too.
