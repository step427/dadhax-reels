The 10 Principles of Neuroplasticity

Your nervous system rewires by experience. These ten principles (Kleim & Jones, 2008) drive real recovery in the clinic — and they apply to changing any habit or belief, because it's the same biology.

  1. Use it or lose it.Neural circuits you stop using degrade. Skills, strength, beliefs — all of them.
  2. Use it and improve it.Training a function strengthens the circuits behind it. Practice literally rebuilds the wiring.
  3. Specificity.You get better at exactly what you practice. Train the thing itself, not a lookalike.
  4. Repetition matters.A few tries change nothing. Rewiring takes reps — often hundreds. Hearing a new belief ~100 times is what makes your system buy it.
  5. Intensity matters.Weak, casual practice produces weak change. Effortful practice drives adaptation.
  6. Time matters.Plasticity unfolds in windows. Consistency over weeks beats cramming, always.
  7. Salience matters.The brain rewires for what it cares about. The rep has to matter to you.
  8. Age matters.Younger brains adapt faster — but plasticity never hits zero. Older just means more deliberate reps.
  9. Transference.Training one skill can strengthen related ones. Wins spill over.
  10. Interference.Plasticity cuts both ways — practicing the wrong pattern wires that in instead. Reps of bad habits are still reps.
The takeawayWhether you're recovering from a stroke or rebuilding what you believe about yourself: do the reps, make them intense, make them matter, give them time. About 20 hours of dedicated focus on one thing puts you in the top 5%. The work is the way.
