SHEET 06 // INTERROGATION // THE CLOCK IS HIS

Outwit the Devil

He's at the table. You get five minutes. He has to answer everything — but he only has to answer exactly what you ask. Ask yes/no questions and he'll burn your clock one word at a time. Ask better questions and he starts confessing.

WIN: flip all 7 confessions · LOSE: the clock, your temper, or both

This game asks you for nothing. No gate on the rest of the site, no email, no analytics. Everything you type stays in this tab and dies with it. He'd love to know your fears — we don't collect them.
Why this one exists

I've been listening to Napoleon Hill interrogate the devil on my drives — written 1938, locked away until 2011 — and I keep getting caught mid-sentence: yep, guilty of that. This is that chair, turned around. Your turn.

What you're talking to: Hill's devil isn't theology — he's an allegory with a voice: fear and drift, personified so you can cross-examine them. The one at this table is scripted — a sparring dummy wearing the costume. The real one doesn't use a script, which is exactly why the practice matters.

The brief — read before you sit
  1. He is holding seven cards — his secrets. The face-down cards below are his hand: how he actually takes people. Your job is to make him confess every one before the clock dies. Each confession flips a card face-up.
  2. Your only crowbar is the open question. WHAT, HOW, WHY — aimed at how he works, what he uses, what he's afraid of. Press a thread and he has to give it up.
  3. He scores three ways: yes/no questions (one-word answer, your clock burns) · your temper (every degree you heat up, he cools down) · the clock itself. The seventh card is locked until you've earned it.
