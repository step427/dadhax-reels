Hey Dad,

If a cleat hanger still shifts when you grab the tool, make a small wedge and push it in behind the stop. That locks it down.

The stop keeps the load pushing into the wall instead of prying the hanger off it. The wedge takes out the last bit of slop. Mine's a thin strip of wood; the offcuts from ripping your 45s are right there.

Thu Sep 246:00prepostThree answers before shellac, then 220 grit between coats 
Hey Dad,

Finishing is where a good build gets ruined, and I went through three answers before I got there.

Dark stain on a sample: no. Matte poly: no. Shellac: yes.

Then it is just patience. Underside first at zero coats. Topside after that, 220 grit sanding between every single application. Six coats before I stopped.

Sanding between coats is the whole trick. It knocks down the dust that landed while the last coat dried and gives the next one something to grab.

A top made of eight 45 degree wedges shows every flaw you leave in it, so the finish has to be boring and repeatable.

Nobody films the boring half. I post it anyway, every day. Follow and you get it.
