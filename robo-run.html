Robo Run

A lot of the free games on your kid's tablet are built to keep them playing: a video ad that cuts into the round, a cartoon character nudging them to buy coins.

This one has three short levels (a run, a soccer match and a kart race), then it says THE END. It has no ads, nothing to buy and no sign-up. You can build one like it with your kid tonight.

Nothing on this page watches your kid play. Once it loads, it makes no outside calls, runs no ads or analytics, and saves nothing. The robot is drawn with rectangles, and your browser makes every beep on the spot.

We made this on a tangent while mocking up our basement remodel. I was building a 3D walk-through of the plan with an AI, and the laptop on the desk in it opened a tiny game. I'm a physical therapist and I don't write code. The AI wrote every line.

When I asked whether the game could live on this site, it got three levels and a start button. After my playtests, level two became soccer and level three a kart race. The whole game sits in one file, and the prompt below asks an AI for the same kind of file, built around your kid's ideas.

Build one with your kid

Open whatever AI chat you already use and paste this in. Let your kid answer its questions. Some AI chats can run the game right in the chat window. If yours can't, save the answer on a computer as game.html and open it in a browser.

My kid and I want to build a tiny video game together tonight, and neither of us knows how to code. My kid is [your kid's age] years old.

Step 1: Ask my kid three questions, one at a time, and wait for each answer. Who is the hero? What does the hero collect? What does the hero have to dodge?

Step 2: Write the whole game as ONE html file we can save and open in a web browser. No images, no libraries, nothing loaded from the internet: draw everything with simple shapes on a canvas. Arrow keys and space to play, plus big on-screen buttons so it works on a phone. Three short levels, then a THE END screen. Keep the code short and simple.

Step 3: After the code, show us one number we can change ourselves, like how high the hero jumps, and quote the exact line it's on.

A good answer is one block of code that starts with <!DOCTYPE html>, ends with </html>, and works the first time we open it. If it doesn't, we'll paste the error back to you.
