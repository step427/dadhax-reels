The sheet plan

You've got a project and a list of parts. You're standing in the lumber aisle doing math on your phone, and you're not sure if it's two sheets or three.

Guess low and you're back at the store Saturday afternoon for one more sheet. Guess high and a hundred-dollar sheet leans on your garage wall for a year.

Type your parts and you get the plan: how many sheets to buy, which strips to rip off each one, and where every crosscut goes. A garage shelf unit, 72 tall and 16 deep with five shelves and a kick: three sheets. Make it 15 7/8 deep and it's two. The blade's eighth is what costs you the third.

Nothing you type here leaves your device. No account, no server, no analytics. Your numbers live in this browser tab and are erased when you close it. "Start over" wipes them now.

Why this one exists

I wasted a lot of plywood learning this the other way. Now every project starts the same: pencil and grid paper, the measurements, the stock I already have, then the cut list. For the full layout I use a free optimizer, and it's better at it than I am. This page is the five-minute version you can run in the aisle: the sheet count and the rip order. Every board you don't waste is one you don't buy.

Rip first

Every part rides a full-length strip as wide as the part. Rip the strips, then crosscut them. Fewer fence moves, and the long cut is the easy one to get straight.

The blade's eighth

Three 16-inch strips are 48 inches. A sheet is 48 inches. The two cuts between them eat a quarter inch, and the third strip comes up short. The page counts every kerf.

Part 1 — Your parts

One part per line: how many, length, width, and a name if you want it. Fractions are fine. The longer side runs with the 8-foot grain.

Filled with the shelf unit so you can see it work. Clear it and type yours, or paste a list. A semicolon works as a new line.

Part 2 — The stock

A full 4x8 sheet and a 1/8 blade unless you change them. Measure your sheet if you're cutting it close.

Part 3 — Your plan

Type at least one part above and the plan appears here.
Your sheets, step by step

this page doesn't nest parts It rips first and never mixes widths on one strip, so it can overcount by a sheet. For a true layout, put the same list into cutlistoptimizer.com, free.
The boulder question

Most problems are who-you-know. Name one person who'd hold the far end of the sheet. Breaking down plywood is a good third thing: one of you on each end, and you talk while you work.

Now make the call — ask: “got an hour Saturday? I'll buy lunch.”
