The wound map

You keep landing in the same spot: the same fight, or the same week where you're fixing everything and nobody asked. You try harder and it comes back. The move that kept you safe a long time ago is still running, and now it costs you.

Tap the voice you know. Tap how you protect it. Under a minute, nothing to type. You walk out with what you've been chasing, what it could turn into, and one thing to do by next week. 11 voices, 3 moves. Mine is already filled in below: "I am not enough," and I went invisible.

Nothing you tap here leaves your device. No account, no server, no analytics. Your picks are erased when you close the tab.

this page isn't a diagnosis I'm a physical therapist, not your therapist. This is a map I pulled together from other people's work. Nobody has tested it as a measure, and it can't tell you who you are. If a row brings up something heavy, stop here and talk to someone licensed. In crisis or thinking about hurting yourself? In the US, call or text 988, any hour. In danger right now, call 911.
Why this one exists

I let an AI ask me 20 hard questions on camera. In my hardest season, what emotion did I live in? "My worthiness. Am I worthy of this?" Then I tried to name the feeling and couldn't. Naming emotions, I said, is "oddly just very hard," and I asked it for a list. So this page is a list.

As a kid: "I had to become invisible." Grown: "Often I am told I am too much," and in one low chapter "I tried to be little. It was not fun." Then the shop: "Maybe that's why DIY dad stuff is so appealing to me, because I'm fixing something." What I do about it is a line I'd already posted. The person you're best suited to help is the person you were five years ago: Instagram, Facebook.

Your map

The boulder question

Most problems are who-you-know. Name one person who has seen you at your worst and stayed.

Now make the call. You don't owe them the map. Ask: “when do you see me go quiet, push, or try to fix it?”

Where this comes from: three directions of protecting, Karen Horney and schema therapy's coping styles; the need turned outward, Alfred Adler and altruism born of suffering; purpose from gifts too, McAdams and Guo. The grid itself is my synthesis.
